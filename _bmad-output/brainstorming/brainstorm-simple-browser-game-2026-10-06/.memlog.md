- (direction) Scope constraint: must be ready in 2-3 days; browser-based; primary aim is learning, secondary is fun for other players
- (decision) Stance: Facilitator. Technique batch (facilitator chosen): Crazy 8s, Trait Transfer, What If Scenarios, Ship in 60 Minutes
- (decision) Re-composed on the page: stance changed to Creative Partner. New batch: Explain It to a Golden Retriever (random pick), then 1 invented technique, then 1 coach-chosen technique. Earlier facilitator batch superseded.
- (technique) Invented technique: 'Bus Stop Playtest' - pitch the game as something a stranger plays in the 90 seconds before their bus arrives; every idea must survive no instructions, no login, one thumb, and being abandoned mid-play without regret
- (decision) Coach-chosen third technique: Ship in 60 Minutes (constraint) - fits the 2-3 day deadline
- (technique) started Explain It to a Golden Retriever
- (idea by coach) Tap falling snacks before they hit the floor, faster and faster; no story, levels or menus
- (idea by user) Mutation: a squirrel catching falling acorns instead of snacks - the game has a character and a theme now
- (idea by coach) Rotten acorns: catching one gives the squirrel a stomach ache and slows it for a few seconds - introduces things to avoid
- (idea by user) Other falling objects: leaves and branches
- (question) What do leaves and branches DO when caught - still undefined
- (idea by coach) Leaves drift sideways, slow and worthless; they block the view of acorns behind them - decoys, not threats
- (idea by user) Branches must be dodged; a branch that hits the squirrel knocks it down
- (insight) Three object classes emerging: collect (acorns), avoid (rotten acorns, branches), ignore/obscure (leaves)
- (idea by coach) Knocked down = squirrel on its back for ~2s, legs kicking; acorns landing in that window bounce off its belly and are lost
- (decision) Game over rule: three hits and you're out (lives system)
- (idea by coach) Lives shown as three acorns in the squirrel's cheeks; each hit pops one out - no hearts or HUD text
- (idea by user) Reward: a visible pile of acorns growing at the base of the tree (score made physical)
- (insight) Golden Retriever survivors: squirrel + falling acorns, rotten acorns slow, branches knock down, leaves obscure, 3 hits out, cheek-acorn lives, growing pile as score. Everything is visual, no text needed.
- (technique) started Bus Stop Playtest (invented)
- (idea by coach) Game-over screen: squirrel climbs down and sits on the pile; pile size IS the end screen, no text
- (decision) Control: drag left and right with one thumb (touch); squirrel moves horizontally along the ground/branch
- (idea by coach) Squirrel follows the thumb with a slight lag so it feels like a creature chasing the finger, not a cursor
- (idea by user) Onboarding without text: the very first acorn falls straight at the squirrel so the player catches it without doing anything and learns catching = good
- (idea by coach) Second acorn falls one thumb-width to the side so the player drags by reflex; two acorns teach the full control scheme
- (idea by user) Branches telegraph with a shadow on the ground before they fall, so the player learns to dodge before the first hit
- (idea by coach) Only dangerous things cast shadows; acorns and leaves do not. Shadow grows as the branch nears. Rotten acorns cast a faint shadow as the sneaky middle case
- (idea by user) Retention/share hook: a friend's pile to beat - the social loop is comparing acorn piles
- (idea by coach) Friend's pile travels in the share URL (score encoded in the link); opens as a faint ghost pile at the base of the tree. No server, accounts or login. Beating it buries the ghost under your real pile
- (idea by user) Ghost pile sits next to the tree; when you pass it the squirrel does a little dance
- (insight) Bus Stop Playtest survivors: drag control with lag, first two acorns teach catch+move, shadows mark danger, ghost pile via URL as the share/retention hook, squirrel dance on overtaking
- (technique) started Ship in 60 Minutes
- (idea by coach) 60-minute version: cut rotten acorns, leaves, ghost pile, dance; fake art with rectangles and circles; borrow nothing beyond a canvas and one JS file
- (decision by user) 60-minute priorities: KEEP the ghost pile (share link with encoded score), cut everything else - the social hook outranks all polish and extra object types
- (idea by coach) 60-minute ghost pile: share = copy link, URL carries the score, ghost drawn as a grey pile at that height - about twenty lines of code
- (decision by user) First thing added back with the 2-3 day budget: real squirrel art (the character is the charm; a rectangle does not make anyone want to share)
- (idea by coach) Art source for a non-artist in 2-3 days: free sprite pack, or scaled emoji (squirrel, acorn) flipped on direction change; real sprites only if time remains on day three
- (decision by user) Definition of done: smooth controls. If the drag does not feel responsive and fluid, the game is not ready to hand to a stranger
- (insight) Ship in 60 Minutes survivors: core loop (drag, catch, dodge, 3 hits, pile) + ghost pile via URL is the MVP; priority after that = squirrel art, then everything else; smooth controls are the quality bar
- (decision by user) Game name: Hungry Squirrel
- (direction) User wants sound in the game; second batch focused on audio
- (technique) started Sensory Exploration (scoped to sound for Hungry Squirrel)
- (idea by coach) Sound: acorn catch is a tiny dry crunch whose pitch rises with each consecutive catch (streak = climbing scale); a miss resets the pitch
- (idea by user) Sound: branch hit = loud thud followed by a squirrel squeak
- (idea by coach) Sound: squeak gets more pathetic per life lost - surprised eek, longer whine, sad deflating squeak on the third as the squirrel climbs down
- (idea by user) Sound: ambient wind in the tree that intensifies as the game speeds up - the wind is the difficulty meter
- (idea by coach) Wind as the in-fiction cause: calm start, gusts drop more acorns, strong gusts snap branches; shadow appears right after a gust sound so players learn gust = look up
- (idea by user) Sound: passing the ghost pile triggers a crowd of other squirrels cheering from the tree during the dance
- (idea by coach) Visual payoff: cheering squirrels briefly poke their heads out of the leaves during the dance
- (idea by user) Sound: game over ends with a contented munch as the squirrel sits on its pile - losing feels like a satisfied ending, not a punishment
- (insight) Sensory Exploration (sound) survivors: rising-pitch crunch streak, thud + escalating squeaks per life, wind as ambience and difficulty meter and cause of falling things, squirrel crowd cheer on beating the ghost, contented munch at game over. All sounds feasible with free SFX or Web Audio synthesis.
- (technique) started Converging: MoSCoW scoping for the 2-3 day build
- (decision by user) MoSCoW agreed as drafted by coach, no changes
- (decision) MUST (day 1): one-thumb drag with follow lag + smooth controls; acorns fall and pile grows; branches with shadow warning knock down; 3 cheek-acorn lives; squirrel sits on pile at game over; ghost pile via share URL + copy-link; placeholder art (emoji/free sprite); speed ramp
- (decision) SHOULD (day 2): real squirrel art flipped on direction; textless tutorial (first acorn straight down, second to the side); core sounds (crunch, thud+squeak, contented munch); dance on passing ghost pile
- (decision) COULD (day 3): rising-pitch streak crunch; escalating squeaks per life; wind ambience rising with speed and gusting before branches; squirrel crowd cheer + heads poking out; knocked-down belly-bounce animation
- (decision) WON'T this time: rotten acorns, leaves, any server/accounts/leaderboard/daily mode - saved for version two
- (insight by user) User's synthesis: the game is really about beating your friend's pile - the social rivalry is the core, the catching is the vehicle
- (insight by coach) Coach synthesis: (1) Every chosen reward is physical and silent - pile, cheek acorns, shadow, dance, munch - so the game needs zero text and works for anyone on any phone, which is what makes a shared link land. (2) Ghost pile + emoji art + Web Audio means zero backend and zero asset pipeline: the whole thing is one HTML file, ideal for learning. (3) The contented munch and the cheering crowd mean losing is kind and winning is social - the two emotional beats a shareable game needs. (4) Wind unifies difficulty, ambience and fiction in one system. Chosen direction: Hungry Squirrel, single-file browser game, MVP = core loop + ghost-pile share link, built over 3 days per the MoSCoW tiers.
